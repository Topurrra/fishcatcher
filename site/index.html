How it protects you

Clear warnings, in words anyone understands.

Every check runs on your device. When something looks off, FishCatcher tells you what and why, so you learn to spot it yourself.

Plain language, not scary jargon

Instead of a red screen, you get a sentence or two you can act on. The exact reasons behind every warning, spelled out.

Domain looks like a misspelling of paypal.com
Uses a high abuse domain ending (.tk)
Asks for a password on a site that is not known

A risk color you read at a glance

The toolbar icon turns one of four colors. You always decide what to do next.

GreenNo suspicious signs.
YellowA little unusual. Double check.
OrangeSeveral indicators. Be careful.
RedStrong signs of a fake. Do not log in.

Checks QR codes

Right click any QR image to read the link inside before you trust it. Fully on your device.

Watches links and downloads

Flags a link whose text hides a different destination, and a download disguised as a safe file.

Runs offline, on your device

The lists it checks against ship inside the extension. No connection needed, nothing sent out.

Three steps, and none of them are yours.

FishCatcher works quietly in the background. You only hear from it when something is worth a second look.

01 / Check

It reads the page

FishCatcher looks at the address and content of the site you are on, entirely on your device.

02 / Warn

It sets a risk color

Lookalike domains, fake login pages, confusing links and known bad sites each raise the level.

03 / Learn

It explains why

Every warning is written plainly, so the next scam is one you recognise on your own.

Your privacy

It works for you, not on you.

FishCatcher does its checks locally, using lists that ship inside the extension. You can verify that by reading the code.

No account, no tracking

Nothing from the pages you visit is sent anywhere. There is no sign up and no profile.

Online checks are opt in

Two optional features can look something up online. Both stay off until you turn them on, and each says exactly what it sends.

Open and verifiable

The whole thing is MIT licensed. The privacy promise is one you can check yourself.
