Fish Catcher vs Bitdefender TrafficLight

Updated 11 August 2026 View source
Honest comparison

Fish Catcher is a free, open-source phishing warner that keeps its default checks on your device; Bitdefender TrafficLight is a free extension from a major antivirus vendor that leans on cloud-based URL and reputation checking.

Local checks versus a vendor cloud

Fish Catcher is built to be local-first. Its default checks run in your browser, nothing about the page you visit leaves the device by default, and there is no account or tracking. When it flags something it shows a plain-language reason and a risk color from green to red. It warns rather than blocks, so you stay in control of the final decision.

Bitdefender TrafficLight is a free browser extension backed by a large security company. As of writing it is generally known for cloud-based checking of the URLs you visit, along with warnings about malware and trackers. That means it can lean on Bitdefender's reputation infrastructure, which is a real advantage for coverage, with the usual trade-off that some data about the pages you visit is typically checked against the vendor's cloud.

Detection depth

Fish Catcher concentrates on signals it can evaluate on-device. It detects lookalike domains and homoglyphs, URL tricks, and fake login forms, and it uses a bundled blocklist plus a daily community feed built from Phishing.Database, URLhaus, and OpenPhish. It adds an on-device model for machine-generated domains, checks for adversary-in-the-middle and device-code scams, ships scam packs for wallet-phrase and fake tech-support pages, and includes a download guard. Optional layers add Google Safe Browsing (with your own key) and a domain-age check. Across 100,000 Majestic Million domains its measured false-positive rate was 0.005%, and that audit is reproducible with a script in the project.

TrafficLight's reach is generally broader on the malware and tracker side because it is part of a full antivirus ecosystem and uses cloud reputation. If your concern extends past phishing into malware-hosting pages and web trackers, a vendor-backed tool typically covers more of that ground than a phishing-focused warner does.

Who should pick which

Pick Fish Catcher if you want your browsing to stay on your device, you want to read the exact reason a page was flagged, and you like that the code and the false-positive audit are open to inspect. Pick TrafficLight if you want cloud-backed coverage that reaches beyond phishing into malware and trackers, and you are comfortable with cloud lookups from a large vendor. The two are not mutually exclusive; a phishing-focused local warner can sit alongside a broader vendor tool.

Where Fish Catcher is weaker

  • It is newer and less battle-tested than a tool from an established antivirus vendor.
  • Its bundled and feed blocklist is smaller than a large commercial reputation database.
  • By default it does not use a cloud reputation service; that is opt-in via Safe Browsing.
  • It focuses on URL and page signals, not full webpage malware scanning, tracker blocking, or network-level protection.

Common questions

Is Fish Catcher a replacement for antivirus?

No. Fish Catcher warns about phishing and scam pages using URL and page signals. It does not scan files on your system or replace an antivirus product. If you want broad malware protection, keep your antivirus and treat Fish Catcher as a focused phishing layer on top.

Does Fish Catcher check my pages against a cloud like TrafficLight does?

Not by default. Its standard checks are on-device. You can turn on the optional Google Safe Browsing layer with your own key if you want an added cloud check, but that is your choice rather than the default behavior.
