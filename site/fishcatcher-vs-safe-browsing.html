Fish Catcher vs built-in Safe Browsing

Updated 19 August 2026 View source
Comparison

Safe Browsing is already inside Chrome and Firefox: it matches the pages you visit against Google’s list of known bad sites and blocks them with a red warning. Fish Catcher is an add-on that reads the page itself and warns, in plain words, before a scam is on anyone’s list.

Which to pick

Keep Safe Browsing on either way. The question is whether to add Fish Catcher beside it.

Pick Fish Catcher if

  • You want a warning that says what looks wrong, not only a red page.
  • You want scams caught before they reach a blocklist.
  • You want the check to stay on your device.

Pick Safe Browsing if

  • You want zero setup.
  • You mainly want known bad pages blocked outright.
  • You are fine with Google seeing partial hashes of what you visit.

Where Fish Catcher is weaker

  • Safe Browsing has years of scale behind it. Fish Catcher is new.
  • Its blocklist is far smaller than Google’s list.
  • It warns but never blocks, so it will not stop a page from loading.
  • By default it uses no cloud reputation service; Safe Browsing inside Fish Catcher is opt-in.

Common questions

If my browser already has Safe Browsing, do I need Fish Catcher?

No, but it covers a different gap. Safe Browsing knows sites that were already reported; Fish Catcher reads the page in front of you and says why it looks risky.

Should I turn off Safe Browsing if I install Fish Catcher?

No, leave it on. Fish Catcher warns and never blocks, so it does not replace the red full-page block.
