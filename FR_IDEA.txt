Realistic challenges
Pure offline detection is hard against 2026 phishing:
•  Many campaigns use very short-lived domains, AI-generated pages, trusted hosting, AiTM techniques, or device-code flows. Local blocklists alone will always lag.
•  Effective offline protection usually needs a combination of techniques: curated local blocklists/bloom filters, URL/domain heuristics, page-content or visual similarity checks, form analysis, and possibly a compact on-device ML model.
•  Users who never opt in to updates will eventually have outdated protection.
•  QR handling adds complexity (decoding images or camera input, extracting the URL, then applying the same checks).
Sustainability with a pure donation model is also difficult long-term, though not impossible if the project stays lightweight and gains a community.
Practical recommendations
•  Make the privacy promise extremely clear and verifiable (open-source the code, document exactly what happens on update, show network activity is empty during normal use).
•  Combine a solid local blocklist with heuristics and lightweight on-device analysis so it still catches many zero-day or short-lived threats even offline.
•  For QR codes: offer both automatic scanning of images on pages/emails and a manual “scan this QR” tool that extracts and checks the URL without sending anything out.
•  Keep the extension lightweight (Manifest V3 constraints, minimal permissions, low resource use).
•  Provide transparent explanations when something is blocked (“This domain is on the local blocklist” or “Suspicious login form detected”) so users understand and trust it.
•  Consider making the core fully offline and any optional cloud reputation check strictly opt-in and clearly labeled.
